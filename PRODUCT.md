# Product brief: SPHEREx TimeLens

**Tagline:** Explore the invisible. Discover what moves.

## Audience

1. **The curious public and students:** no astronomy background; they need plain words and a guided first look.
2. **Hackathon judges and teammates:** they want to see real SPHEREx data working, fast, and honest about its limits.
3. **Amateur and student astronomers:** they want coordinates, wavelengths, uncertainties and exports they can trust.

## Goals

- Make NASA SPHEREx's all-sky infrared survey explorable by anyone: the whole sky first, then any spot over time.
- Show change: things that move (nearby stars, brown dwarfs, asteroids) and things that brighten or fade.
- Stay scientifically honest: real data, correct coordinates, units and errors everywhere, and candidates presented as evidence, not discoveries.

## Main journey

1. **Sky:** open on NASA's real SPHEREx all-sky map in 3D. A short scroll-guided tour visits the five challenge targets, with "Skip to explorer" always available, then free look (drag, zoom).
2. **Choose:** click a target, type a name, or type coordinates. Typed coordinates show the known objects nearby.
3. **Observations:** every SPHEREx exposure of that spot on a real timeline, by release, detector and wavelength.
4. **Compare:** blink, side by side, difference (with uncertainty) and co-add.
5. **Measure:** a spectrum and a light curve at any point, with errors.
6. **Find:** candidate movers and variables with the evidence for each, cross-checked against Gaia and known Solar System objects.
7. **Share:** a link that reproduces the view, plus CSV/JSON exports with data citations.

## Five challenge targets

| Target | What it is |
|---|---|
| Barnard's Star | Red dwarf, 6 light-years away, the fastest-moving star in the sky |
| Epsilon Indi B | A pair of brown dwarfs orbiting the star Epsilon Indi A |
| WISE 0855−0714 | The coldest known brown dwarf (Y dwarf) |
| RW Aurigae | Young variable star (T Tauri binary) |
| R Draconis | Pulsating red giant (Mira variable) |

## Success looks like

- All five targets show real SPHEREx coverage (or an honest explanation where coverage is missing).
- Every number on screen matches an independent check.
- First sky visible within a few seconds on an ordinary connection; nothing blocks the interface.
